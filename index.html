AI then receives 50% of the Avon pension and 3/8 (37.5%) of the Civil Service pension (taxable, still growing at their rates) and his Parmenion pot passes to her — withdrawals are tax-free if death is before age 75, otherwise taxed as her income. Mortality Sensitivity

🪦 If one of them dies, can the survivor still pay the bills — and how much is left over? For every possible year of death (2028–2063, from the year EI takes his pension) the full projection is rerun with the survivor pension, the inherited pot and the reduced living costs applied. Each scenario below answers in one glance: three headline numbers, then one chart — one bar per year of death: the survivor's net income in a typical year, against the reduced living costs shown as a dashed line. A bar rising above the line is a surplus; one falling below it is a deficit. Green = living costs plus the full comfort margin funded every year. Amber = bills covered, but the full comfort margin isn't reached in some years (pot pressure or the 20%-band cap). Red = at least one year where the pots cannot cover the bills. Hover a bar for the exact surplus and the tightest-year figure. One death is modelled at a time — for a specific combination of both deaths, set them in the Mortality Planning sections above and read the Year-by-Year table.

If EI dies — the impact on AI

AI receives 50% of EI's Avon pension and 3/8 (37.5%) of his Civil Service pension every year from his death (shown in her Mort column), and his remaining Parmenion pot transfers into hers — withdrawals tax-free if he dies before 75, taxed as her income at 75+. Family living costs reduce by the mortality-section percentage, and AI's drawdown rises if needed so her net income covers the reduced costs plus her comfort margin.

What this means: as the survivor, AI draws each year the reduced living costs plus this margin (e.g. 20% extra for comfort, gifts and one-off spending) — never drawing so much that her income enters the 40% tax band. If her pots could not keep paying the extra every year until age 90, the model prudently falls back to the living costs only — so money above the bills is actually being spent, not silently left to accumulate, and the remaining-pot figures stay realistic.
